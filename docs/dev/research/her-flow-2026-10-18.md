## Where the 0.4 s goes (2026-10-18, follow-up)

- The rig's hard cap makes it, not the app (HIGH). Without the cap (`--cap 0`) the longest stall is 54-64 ms; with the
  cap and no game, 56-91 ms; only the cap and the game together give 0.3-0.65 s. In one capped check `_reload_history`
  took 379 ms of wall time on 47 ms of CPU: the job was paused partway through. Windows pauses every thread of a job
  that has spent its budget; her PC has no such cap.
- So F1 to marks and the app's share come from the capped rig (`--cap 18 --cpus 2-9,14-17 --game 10 --frame-ms 6`);
  stalls only from a run without the cap. Game frames on time on the capped rig are the rig's too (LOW for her PC).
- No rig without the cap lands on her log. On CPUs 2-9, v1.0.0 spends about 3 CPU-seconds a check whatever the game,
  so share x 8 CPUs x time stays near 3, where hers needs 11-18; on 4 or 6 CPUs it falls off a cliff (9-16 s, OCR
  threads oversubscribed).
- 4 OCR threads against 6 on the capped rig: the same F1 to marks (1.21 s), game frames on time in a check 65 % against
  50 %. Kept 6: those frame numbers are the rig's, and her next log (its "OCR threads" line, check times, CPU) decides.
- The app's own cost before the marks was `_reload_history` (the 记录 list, up to 40 scans): 50-55 ms of CPU every
  check, almost all of `_on_checked`. Since 11db14a the marks come first and the lists are rebuilt only where they
  can be seen: in the tray or on 设置 they wait until shown, in view 100 ms after the marks (a 0 ms timer can run
  before the marks' paint). F1 to marks 1.24 s -> 1.07 s, after 5 s idle 1.25 s -> 0.84 s, 12 names in both (one
  capped run each, in one go, `--checks 5 --idle 5`); more than the 50 ms of CPU, since the cap paused that rebuild
  too. Measured offscreen with 40 records and 350 entries: the rebuild 71-75 ms of wall time, `likes_page.refresh`
  under 1 ms.
